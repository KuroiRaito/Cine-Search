01

The grid

Four pixels. Every dimension in Projector — padding, margin, gap, line-height, control height, icon box — is a multiple of 4. One exception is permitted and only one: 2px, for optical nudges where 4 is visibly too much (an icon sitting a hair low against its label).

Four is the right base for this product rather than eight, because a phone at 320px cannot afford 8px as its smallest real step: a field label 8px above its input and a hint 8px below reads as one undifferentiated block. Four gives the tight rungs the compact layout needs while still halving cleanly into the 16 / 24 / 40 gutters.

The law. A raw pixel value in a module stylesheet is a defect, not a preference. npm run verify rejects any length that is not a token (§15). If a value you need is not on the scale, the scale is wrong — change it here, not locally.

The grid is visible behind every specimen in this document. Where a specimen's edges land on a line, it is on the scale. That is the only proof that matters.

02

Space

Thirteen rungs, rendered below at true size. Tokens are named for their value: --space-16 is 16 pixels. There is no arithmetic to do and nothing to misremember, which matters more than the theoretical ability to re-theme a grid that will never change.

--space-0Collapse a gap deliberately. Never omit the token.
--space-2Optical nudge only. The single sub-grid step.
--space-4Icon to its own label. Tightest real gap.
--space-8Field label to input. Items inside one control.
--space-12Related items in a group. Sheet poster to title.
--space-16Gutter at compact. Field to field. Default.
--space-20Sheet padding. Cover block inset.
--space-24Section to section. Gutter at medium.
--space-32Form block to the next block of a different kind.
--space-40Gutter at expanded. Page top padding, desktop.
--space-48Major break inside a long page.
--space-64Page-level rhythm at expanded.
--space-80Largest. Above the fold on the cover only.
Why 10 and 14 are gone. They were the two most-used values in the codebase after 8 — 31 and 16 uses. Both sit off the 4px grid, and both were arrived at by nudging rather than choosing. Every one snaps to 8, 12 or 16. The gutter moves 14 → 16, which shifts every screen's content edge by two pixels and brings the page into alignment with everything inside it.

Type

Every line-height sits on the 4px grid. That is what makes two columns of different type align at the baseline instead of drifting apart down the page. Roles are named for their job, not their size, because the size changes at expanded and the job does not.

04

Radius

Five rungs replacing eleven. The eleven included 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 — nine values inside a nine-pixel span, none of which a person could tell apart, all of which a developer had to choose between.

--radius-4chips, hints
--radius-8artwork, tiles
--radius-12controls, cards
--radius-16sheets
--radius-fullpills, avatars
Radius encodes scale, not decoration. A bigger surface takes a bigger radius, so the curve reads as constant relative to the box. A 44px button at --radius-16 looks like a lozenge; a 460px sheet at --radius-8 looks square. Pick by the size of the thing, and never by taste.
Nested radius. When one rounded box sits inside another with padding p, the inner radius is the outer minus p. A poster at --radius-8 inside a sheet at --radius-16 with 20px padding is correct because 16 − 20 is already below zero — the inner curve is independent. Where padding is small, the rule bites: never give a child the same radius as its parent.
05

Border, focus and separation

TokenValueWhere
--border-hair1pxEvery border in the product. There is one border weight.
--border-focus2pxFocus ring only. Nothing else is 2px, so a focus ring can never be mistaken for a border.

The focus ring is not optional

Every interactive element shows :focus-visible as a 2px --acc outline at 3px offset. The offset matters: an outline flush to the edge of a filled button disappears into it.

:focus-visible {
  outline: var(--border-focus) solid var(--acc);
  outline-offset: 3px;
  border-radius: var(--radius-4);
}
Never outline: none without a replacement. A keyboard user who cannot see where they are cannot use the form at all. If a design calls for a different focus treatment, it must still be 2px, still --acc, and still visible against both themes.

Three ways to separate, used by role

DeviceSaysUse for
SpaceThese are different things.The default. Reach for this first, always.
HairlineThese are a list of the same thing.Table rows, settings rows, a form's sections.

Not everything is a card. Border, fill, radius and shadow each independently say “separate object”. Spending all four on every block flattens the hierarchy — if everything is lifted, nothing is. A card earns its surface by being independently actionable.

This is the section that fixes the alignment problems. In the current build a button is 42px tall and a text input computes to roughly 43px. Stacked in the sign-up form they are one pixel apart — not enough to read as deliberate, more than enough to look wrong.

TokenHeightPaddingTypeUse
--control-sm36px0 12px--text-labelDense rows, inline actions, expanded-width toolbars. Needs an expanded hit area.
--control-md44px0 16px--text-labelThe default. Every button, input, select and tappable row unless stated otherwise.

The 44-pixel law. Every interactive target is at least 44×44 CSS pixels, including its hit area. A control drawn smaller than 44 — a 36px chip, a 24px close icon — must expand its hit area with a pseudo-element, not by growing the visible box. 
.chip::after {
  content: ""; position: absolute; inset: 50% auto auto 50%;
  width: var(--control-md); height: var(--control-md);
  transform: translate(-50%, -50%);
}

Why 44 and not 48. Apple's guidance is 44, Google's is 48. Projector's densest real screen is the episode list, where 48px rows push a season of 24 episodes past three full phone screens. 44 clears the accessibility floor, matches the platform the product ships on first, and buys back a full screen of scrolling on the screen that needs it most.

Inputs and buttons share a height, always. When a form stacks a field above a submit button, they are the same object to the eye. Both take --control-h. This single rule removes the 1px drift that is currently visible on the sign-up screen.
07

Icons

TokenBoxPaired withUse
--icon-1616px--text-caption, --text-body-smInline with a label. Tab bar. Field affixes.
--icon-2020px--text-body, --text-labelButton leading icons. List row leading icons.
--icon-2424px--text-title-sm and abovePage-head actions. Standalone icon buttons.
The set is Lucide, chosen 2026-09-16. One 24px grid, one 2px stroke, ISC licence, and lucide-react-native exists for the native milestone. Thirty-three icons ship; measured, they cost 3.8 KB gzipped over the bundle without them. Roles are named for what the product calls them — discover, want, rewatched — so the drawing can change without touching a screen. The map is src/shared/ui/Icon.jsx and named imports keep it tree-shaken.
An icon is a component, never a character. Before this the product drew every icon with a Unicode glyph borrowed from whatever block had roughly the right shape — twenty of them across five unrelated blocks. Painted at one 100px font size they ranged from 10px to 86px tall with 48px of drift between optical centres, which is why nothing lined up. Search was U+2315 TELEPHONE RECORDER, painting 44px beside a discover icon painting 86. npm run verify now rejects a short text node or string literal containing one.

The same four tabs after the change: 20×20 each, zero drift, one stroke weight.
The box is square and fixed; the drawing inside it is not. Optical sizing still differs by shape — a circle at 20px reads slightly smaller than a square at 20px. Size the box from the token so layout stays predictable, and let the artwork sit where it sits.
A 2px stroke does not survive being scaled down. On a 24 grid it becomes 1.33px at 16, which lands between pixels and goes soft. Icon lifts the stroke to 2.25 at 16px rather than shipping a second icon set.
The rating star is filled. The rating control is filled only as far as it is filled.

Lucide's star is a stroke icon, so it draws hollow by default — and a hollow star is the empty half of a rating. Using it to display a score says the opposite of what is meant: it is the difference between rated 9 and rate this.

Two different jobs, two different rules:
• Displaying a score — always fill="currentColor", stroke lifted to 1.5. Star and number side by side, never stacked.
• The rating control — a chosen star is filled, an unchosen one is hollow. That is the whole affordance, and it is what makes the outline mean something.

08

Breakpoints

The codebase has eleven: 48, 220, 320, 400, 430, 480, 600, 700, 760, 1100, 1120. Four of those are real layout boundaries and the rest are one component each, guessed at in isolation. Worse, two pairs nearly collide — and that near-collision is a live bug.

The stretching you can see on a wide window. --gutter steps at 1100px in shell.css, but every component reflows at 1120px in its own module. Between 1100 and 1120 the page uses the 40px desktop gutter while the layout inside it is still tablet. The same fault exists at 700 / 760. Two files disagreeing about where desktop begins is exactly what “things stretch, things go short” looks like.

Above 1440 the gutter stops being what positions anything

This is the part the first version of this document got wrong: expanded had no upper bound, so a 1920px monitor was simply an unstated case. It is stated now. The container caps at 1600 and the margin absorbs everything past it — the 40px gutter is still there, but it is a minimum inset inside the container, not the thing holding content off the screen edge.

These are measured, not derived. Every number in the Grid column comes from reading grid-template-columns back out of the browser at that viewport, because the arithmetic has a term that is easy to drop: .grid has gap: var(--space-16), so n columns spend (n − 1) × 16px on gaps before any tile gets a pixel. An earlier version of this table was computed at a 14px gap and ran about 2px high on every row.
The container steps; it does not flow. The obvious design is to let content grow continuously from 1120 to the cap, using every pixel. It was built that way first, and it was wrong. .rail hides its scrollbar — scrollbar-width: none, ::-webkit-scrollbar { height: 0 } — and has no arrows, so the partial tile at the edge is the only thing that says a row scrolls, on a mouse exactly as much as on a thumb. A container that flows sweeps the visible tile count through whole numbers: at a 1280px window it landed on 6.06 — six tiles and a twelve-pixel sliver, which reads as a complete set. Two fixed content widths can be checked. A continuum cannot.
Why the grid steps at 1440 and the gutter does not. Six columns is a content ladder, not a band: five columns held to the 1600 cap would put poster tiles at 291px, where a row reads as a gallery wall rather than a set you can compare. A tile stays roughly 180–260px. The gutter, meanwhile, is done growing at 40 — an inset wider than that stops reading as breathing room and starts reading as a missing sidebar.
Breakpoints cannot be custom properties. CSS does not permit var() inside a media query, so 600 and 1120 are written literally. This is the one place raw numbers are legal — and the one place verify checks hardest, because nothing in the language can stop a fourth breakpoint appearing.

320 is the floor, and it is a real device

The iPhone SE first generation and Android Go devices report 320px. At that width the compact gutter leaves 288px of content. Every screen is designed against that number, not against 390. A layout that only works at 390 will clip on hardware people actually own.

Content decides a breakpoint, never a device. If a line of copy wraps badly at 720px, the fix is --measure or a shorter line — not a new breakpoint at 720. Four bands is the budget, and the fourth earns its place by answering a question the other three could not: what happens when the screen keeps growing and the content should not.

Three containers, by what they hold

A single max-width cannot serve a catalogue. Browse surfaces grow; reading surfaces do not.

Elevation

Two shadows, because there are two things that lift: a sheet, and everything else.

TokenFor
--shadow-raisedToasts, menus, a card lifted on hover at expanded.

The scrim does not have a light variant. A scrim's job is to push a layer back, and a pale wash over a pale page does not. This is the same principle as artwork has no theme — the scrim is closer to artwork than to surface.

Component tokens

The layer between atoms and components. A component never reaches for an atom directly when a role exists — it asks for --gutter, and the role decides which atom that is at this width. This is what makes the system adapt to a platform instead of being redrawn for one.

RolecompactmediumexpandedMeans
--gutter16px24px40pxDistance from the viewport edge to any content. One number, obeyed by everything.
--control-h44px44px44pxHeight of every button, input and select. Does not change — a finger does not get smaller on a tablet.
--radius-control12pxShared by buttons and inputs so a stacked form reads as one object.
--field-gap8pxLabel to its input. Input to its hint or error.
--field-stack16pxOne complete field to the next.

Read the --control-h row again. It is constant across all four bands, and that is the point of having a role layer. Without it, somebody looks at a desktop mockup, decides the buttons look chunky, and shrinks them — breaking touch targets on the tablet that shares the breakpoint.

Seven components. Every one is assembled from §2–§9 and nothing else; each specimen below is live HTML using the real tokens, so what you see is what the numbers produce.

Supabase's messages are not a stable contract and its codes are; matching on wording meant a reworded message silently became “Something went wrong”. Every code in src/modules/entry/messages.js was read off a live response from this project, and the copy above is what shipped — not what was designed. Where the two differed, the verified string won.
The password rule is 8 characters, not 6. The form promised 6 and Supabase demanded 8, so the only way to learn the real rule was to fail at it. It is now a live checklist under the field — five requirements that tick green as you satisfy them — rather than a sentence you read after being refused. PASSWORD_RULES in src/modules/entry/rules.js is the single source, and it must track the Supabase dashboard policy.
Typed values survive every error. Except the password after a failed sign-in, which is cleared — that one is a security convention people expect. Everything else stays: making somebody retype an email because the network dropped is the app blaming them for its own problem.
Never leak the taxonomy. On sign-in, a wrong password and an unknown email produce the same message. Distinguishing them tells an attacker which addresses have accounts.

Enforcement

A design system that is only a document decays at the first deadline. The thirty font sizes did not arrive through carelessness — they arrived because nothing rejected them. scripts/verify.mjs already exists and already enforces module ownership of class names; these five rules extend it — and npm run verify now runs thirteen.

CheckRuleDetects
token-scaleIn a converted module, no raw px on a spacing or sizing property. Container caps and blur radii are exempt — they have no scale.The whole class of drift. The one rule that makes the rest hold. CONVERTED = ['entry'] is also the migration board.
gutter-bands--gutter steps only at 600 and 1120, and --shell-max only at 1120 and 1440 — each must step at both of its own.Exactly the 1100/1120 fault. A component may still carry its own content-driven ladder; the page gutter may not.
control-colourA rule setting background on a button, input, select or textarea also sets color.The invisible-in-dark-mode failure that shipped once on .n-pchip.
focus-ringA block with outline:none must draw a replacement in the same block — a box-shadow ring or a border.Silent keyboard inaccessibility. Judged per rule, not per file, so an inset ring counts and a bare removal does not.
layer-tokenz-index is a layer token, or a single digit. Nothing between.Stacking wars. 0–9 is local stacking inside one component's own context; the app's shared layers start at 10 and must be named.
The snapshot check is the second half. npm run snap -- entry --check already renders the module at three widths in both themes and compares fingerprints. V1–V6 stop a wrong number; the snapshot stops a wrong result. Migrating the gutter from 14 to 16 will fail that check on every screen — which is correct, and is the proof the token reaches what it claims to.
The inline-style rule already existed. verify's No inline styles outside Skeleton check covers style={{…}} carrying a length, so it is not repeated here. Five new rules, not six.
A check that cannot fail is worse than no check. All five of these passed on their first run — because verify.mjs aggregates failures into a map before reporting, and the new checks had been appended after that aggregation. They ran, they recorded failures, and the reporter never looked. Every one of them was proven by injecting a real violation and watching it fail. Do that for the next rule too.

What a contract looks like

From the rail-tile correction, and it generalises: a contract records the ladder and the principle, never a lone number. “Rail tiles are 104px” invited the next developer to change it to 128 and be right. The full form is:

Rail tile width: 128 / 148 / 168 / 190 at 320 / 400 / 600 / 1120.
Principle: never a whole number of tiles. A rail has no scrollbar and
no arrow on touch, so the partial tile is the only scroll affordance.
At 375px: 104px shows 3.17 tiles and reads as a complete set;
          128px shows 2.61 and reads as a row that continues.
If a rule cannot say what breaks when it is violated, it is not finished. Every number in this document carries its reason for exactly that purpose — so the next person changes it deliberately, or not at all.
