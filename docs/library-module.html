02

The scale problem

A library is the one screen that only grows. Everything else in this product shows a slice of the catalogue; this shows everything you have ever recorded, and it is the screen most likely to still be in use in five years.

What the reference does at scale

The AL-chan account benchmarked for the profile has 1,497 titles. Its list page, measured on a 430×900 phone:

document height  141,660px  =  157 screens of scroll
14 sections, grouped by status × format:
    Completed TV       772 entries
    Completed Movie     156
    Completed OVA       136
    Completed Special    63
    Completed Music       4
    Chinese               1     (a custom list)
Two failures, and they pull in opposite directions. One section holds 772 entries, which is not a section, it is the whole problem wearing a heading. Another holds one, which is a heading wearing nothing. Grouping does not rescue a list this size — it only decides where the scroll happens.

What ours does

Better in one way and worse in another. Filtering to a single status is already the thing AL-chan will not do, so our longest page is one status rather than all of them. But:

AtRows rendered on “All”What happens
20 titles20Works. This is what the module was designed against.
200 titles200One query, 200 rows in the DOM, a long scroll with no way to jump.
500 titles500Every poster requested at once. Finding a known title means scrolling for it.
1,000+silently truncatedPostgREST applies a server-side row ceiling and Supabase's default is 1,000. loadEntries() sets no range, so past that the library quietly stops being complete.
The truncation is the one to fix first, and it is not a design problem. A library that silently drops your oldest thousandth title is worse than one that is slow: slow is visible. Flagged for the architecture agent — the fix is a range and a count, not a screen. Worth confirming the actual ceiling on this project rather than taking the default on trust.
Two thousand is not hypothetical for this product. A Letterboxd export is the obvious way someone arrives with a history, and a decade of watching is four figures. The profile document already names the imported library as state X1 for the same reason.
03

Find, sort, density

Three controls, in the order they earn their place. Each is one line in the header; none of them is a new screen.

Find — the one that matters most

Not search. Find: a filter over what is already loaded, matching as you type, no request. Search goes to the catalogue and answers “does this exist”; find stays home and answers “where did I put it”. Confusing them is why some apps make you leave your own library to get back to it.

Find collapses the scale problem for the commonest task. The reason somebody opens a 500-title library is usually one title. Four characters gets them there, and the 157-screen question never arises. It is also the cheapest of the three to build — the rows are already in memory.
It matches the title only, and it matches loosely. Not notes, not people, not genre — those are searches and they belong to Search. Diacritic-insensitive and word-prefix, so poor finds Poor Things and ame finds Amélie. A find that misses because of an accent is worse than no find.

Sort

SortAnswersDefault for
Recently updated“what was I doing”Watching, and the current hard-coded behaviour
Recently added“what did I just save”Want to watch
Title A–Z“where is the one I am thinking of”—
Your rating“what did I love”Watched
Release year“what era is this shelf”—
Progress“what is nearly finished”— series only
The default changes with the filter, and that is the point. “Recently updated” is right for Watching and wrong for Watched, where nobody is asking what they touched last — they are asking what was good. A sort that is correct on one shelf and pointless on the next should not be one global setting.
Remembered per filter, not globally. Sorting Watched by rating should not reorder Watching the next time it is opened. Six shelves, six remembered sorts — and none of them survive a sign-out, because they are a preference, not a record.

Density

AL-chan offers three. Ours needs two, because the films-grid / series-rows split already does half the work a density control does elsewhere.

ModeSeriesFilmsRows per screen at 320×600
Comfortable (today)Row with poster, progress track and +3-up poster grid~7 series, ~9 films
Compact (new)One line: status stripe, title, 11/62, scoreOne line, same shape~16
Compact is the same list, not a lesser one. It keeps the status colour as a stripe, the progress as a fraction and the score as a number — everything the comfortable row says except the artwork. Somebody scanning 200 completed films does not need 200 posters; somebody browsing what to watch next does.
Density is a preference and never a default that changes itself. No “we switched you to compact because your library got big”. The one thing a record must be is predictable.

L11 is the module's best existing rule, drawn. The tick landed, the write failed, the row went back to E6 and said so by name — “Couldn't save E7”, not “something went wrong”. A silent rollback is the worst outcome available: the person believes they are one episode further along than the record says, and they find out months later.
L12's remove is footer-isolated and confirms. Every other control in the sheet is reversible in one tap; remove is the only one that destroys a record, so it sits apart from the others, below Save, in --st-drop-11, and asks. That is already how it ships.

A two-hour film is never “watching”.

Series left, films right — not stacked. On a phone they are two sections you scroll past in order. Here they are two columns, because the whole point of one filter holding both is that you can see the shape of a shelf at once: three series in progress and no films is a fact about how somebody watches, and stacking hides it.
The series column is fixed at 420px and the films column fills. A series row is a fixed composition — poster, two lines, a track, a button — and stretching it to 900px leaves the title stranded from its +. Posters have no such problem, so the grid takes the slack.

What the design needs to exist

Requirements, not a schema — how any of it is stored is the architecture agent's call.

NeedsShapeBecause
A bounded readA range and a total countThe only item here that is a defect rather than a feature. Today the whole library is one unbounded query and the server caps it silently. L14 cannot even be drawn honestly until the screen knows the list is partial.
FindClient-side over loaded rows; diacritic-insensitive, word-prefixNo request. The rows are already in memory, which is what makes this the cheapest of the three controls.
SortSix orders; the chosen one remembered per shelfA preference, not a record — it does not survive sign-out and does not sync.
DensityTwo modes, rememberedSame: a preference. Never changes itself.
Progress for a seriesWatched count and total, both excluding specialsAlready correct in episodesWatched() — counting specials on one side of the fraction reads “63 of 62” and draws a bar past its own end.
The next unwatched episodeIts season, number and nameThe + names what it will mark. “Add one to a number” is not the same feature.
07

Decisions

#DecisionRecommendation
LD1R7, still formally open. Films get three statuses and series get six. The code already does this; grooming still lists it as undecided.Ratify what ships. “Watching” on a two-hour film is noise, and the database still permits all six so an import is never rewritten. This needs recording, not deciding — the risk is somebody reading the open question and 'fixing' the code.
LD2Find, sort, density — all three, or start with one?Find first, alone. It is the cheapest, it solves the commonest task, and it makes the scale problem stop hurting while the bounded read is built. Sort second. Density last — it is the one people ask for and the one they use least.
LD3Custom lists. AL-chan has them — the benchmarked account keeps “Summer Holiday 2019” and “Chinese” alongside the six statuses.Not now, and possibly never. Two of that account's fourteen sections are custom lists holding one and sixteen entries. They are a filing system for people who file. Six statuses plus find plus sort answers the same questions without asking anybody to maintain a taxonomy.
LD4What happens at the row ceiling? Truncate silently is today's answer.Page it, and say the count. “203 titles” in the header is cheap and it is the only thing that makes a truncation visible. A library that quietly stops being complete is the worst failure this module can have.
Nothing here is built. This is design, for grooming — except the audit in §01, which is what already ships and was read from the code.
