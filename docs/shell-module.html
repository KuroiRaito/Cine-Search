Shell

The five designed modules each own a screen. Nobody owns what is around them — the tabs, the top bar, the banner that says your session ended, the moment between two routes, and what happens when a screen throws. This is the last surface without a document, it is on every screen in the product, and auditing it turned up eight things, two of which are the kind you only find by reading the code.

What the shell owns, and what it got wrong

Read from the code rather than from a description of it: App.jsx, app/shell.css, NotFound, About, Toast, ThemeToggle, useRegion, and Settings.

What is already right, and should survive this document

Seven of the eight are one mistake repeated: the shell has no opinion about anything that is not layout. It sizes the page beautifully — four bands, one container, a gutter that steps twice — and then leaves failure, waiting, identity, region and feedback to whichever module happens to hit them first. Everything below is the same move applied seven times: if it appears on more than one screen, the shell owns it.
02

The chrome gap, and why the obvious fix is wrong

At 1120 and above there is a top bar: wordmark, four nav links, theme toggle, and either a username or Sign in. Below 1120 there is none of it. The tab bar is the entire shell.

BandTop barTabsWhere sign-in and theme live
compact · 320–599nonebottom, four acrossOnly in Discover's own header.
medium · 600–1119nonebottom, centredOnly in Discover's own header.
expanded · 1120+sticky, fullhiddenThe top bar — and Discover's header, both at once
So the find document is wrong where it says “.head-actions — delete”. That instruction is correct about the duplication at desktop and catastrophic below it: deleting those three controls today would remove the only sign-in affordance and the only theme control from every phone. The instruction is retracted here and replaced by the ruling below. This is exactly the sort of thing grooming is for, and it took reading the shell to find it.

The obvious fix is a mobile header. I do not recommend it.

A sticky 56px bar with a wordmark and two controls would close the gap and cost the top 9% of a 600px-tall screen on every single page, permanently, to hold two things almost nobody touches twice.

ControlWho needs it, and how oftenWhere it should live
WordmarkNobody, on a phone. The app is the whole screen and the tab bar names where you are.Nowhere below 1120. It is branding on a surface that needs no branding.
ThemeOnce, ever, for most people.Settings — where it already is, reachable from the You tab in two taps.
Sign inOnce per device. Critical when needed and invisible the rest of the time.The You tab, which is a permanent, labelled, always-visible destination that already exists.
The ruling: the You tab is the account surface below 1120; the top bar is the account surface above it. Neither duplicates the other, and there is no mobile header. Then .head-actions can be deleted for the right reason rather than the wrong one — not because the top bar duplicates it, but because the tab bar already carries the only two things it held.
One condition attaches to that, and it is checkable. The You tab's guest state currently offers Create an account and nothing else. A returning person on a new device wants Sign in, and today Discover's header is the only place that word appears below 1120. Before .head-actions is deleted, the You tab's guest state must offer both — create, and sign in — as two distinct controls. That is one line of design and it is the whole price of the ruling.
03

Navigation, and the two things it forgets

Four tabs — Discover, Search, Library, You — shown only on those four routes. That much is right and stays. What is missing is everything that should happen between two routes.

L1 — a push scrolls to the top; a pop restores where you were. Today neither happens, so opening a title from halfway down Discover lands you halfway down the title page, and coming back puts you at the top of a list you had scrolled through. Both halves matter and they are different mechanisms: new destination, new scroll position; back, the position you left. The library and the search grid are the two screens where the second one is felt, and the find document already requires it as state FQ26.
L2 — a route change moves focus and says the name of the page. Today focus stays wherever it was, which for somebody using a screen reader means a tab press that silently replaces the page. On navigation, focus moves to the page's h1 (made programmatically focusable, never a tab stop) and a polite live region announces the route's name. This is the single largest accessibility gap in the product and it is about fifteen lines.
The 404 keeps its tabs. Today isTopLevel is false for an unknown path, so the not-found page has no tab bar and exactly one button. A page that exists to recover from a wrong turn is the worst place to remove the navigation — it gets the tabs, and keeps the button.

What About is for

A 52-line static page carrying the TMDB attribution, which the terms require, and the JustWatch credit that watch-provider data requires. It is linked from Settings and nowhere else. That is enough — it is a legal obligation with a link, not a destination, and giving it a nav entry would be the app talking about itself on a screen where somebody is trying to find a film.

04

When a screen throws

Today: the whole application unmounts. React's default for an uncaught render error is to tear the tree down, and with nothing catching it the person is left on a white page with no tabs, no wordmark and no route — the app has not crashed visibly, it has simply stopped existing. Only a refresh brings it back, and a refresh returns to the same route and the same crash.

L3 — a failure is contained by the smallest thing that can contain it. Three levels, and the product already has the third.
LevelCatchesWhat survivesStatus
1 · SectionA failed request inside one band — a rail, the people row, a seasonEverything. The band shows its own retry.Shipped — ErrorBox, used well
2 · RouteA module that throws while renderingThe shell. Tabs, top bar and every other route still work; only the content area is replaced.Missing
3 · AppA throw in the shell itselfNothing, but the person is told, and given a reload rather than a blank page.Missing

What the route-level screen says

PartContentWhy
The line“This screen stopped working.”Not “Something went wrong”, which is true of everything and useful for nothing. It says which thing: this screen, not your data, not the app.
The reassurance“Nothing you saved is affected.”The first fear on a tracker is the library. Answer it before it is asked.
Two actionsTry again · Go to DiscoverRetry re-mounts the route, which fixes a transient throw. The second is the way out when it does not, and it is a route the shell knows is safe.
A referenceA short error id in mono, small, at the bottomSo a report can name the failure. Never the stack — a stack trace on screen tells a person nothing and tells them loudly.
The tabsStill there.This is the entire point of catching at the route: the rest of the product is fine, and the screen should prove it.
The boundary must reset on navigation. An error boundary that keeps its error after the route changes turns one broken screen into a permanently broken tab — you tap Library, get the failure, tap Discover, and get the same failure. Keying it to the pathname is the whole fix, and forgetting it is the classic way this feature ships broken.
05

Waiting

There are three waits in this product and the shell currently designs none of them.

WaitHow longTodayDesign
The route's codeOne network round trip, once per module per sessionfallback={null} — nothing at allThe shell stays — tabs, top bar, and the page's own heading where it is known — and the content area carries three skeleton blocks. Never a spinner in an empty page.
The route's dataOne or two TMDB callsSkeletons, per module, well doneUnchanged. This is the one that already works.
The sessionMilliseconds, usually; seconds on a cold startHandled: authReady gates the account control so nobody is offered Sign in for a second before their name appearsUnchanged, and worth naming as the model the other two should copy: three states, never two.
L4 — the shell never disappears while something loads. A lazy route that paints nothing is indistinguishable from a crash, and the difference between the two is the only thing the person cares about. Whatever else is happening, the tabs stay on screen: they are the proof that the app is still there and the way out if it is not.
And the skeleton is the shell's, not the module's. One fallback for every lazy route, because the shell cannot know which module is arriving — three blocks at the grid's own geometry, which is close enough to every screen in the product that nothing jumps more than a few pixels when the real thing lands.
06

Region, theme, and a third party nobody agreed to

The region

useRegion runs on first load and, before anybody has tapped anything, does this:

const res = await fetch('https://ipapi.co/json/');
…
const browserLang = navigator.language;        // only if that fails
The visitor's IP address is sent to a company this product has no relationship with. No consent, no mention anywhere in the interface, no entry in any document. The Settings hint says the region was “detected from your connection”, which is true about the effect and silent about the means.

Keep the accuracy. Drop the stranger.

Where-to-watch needs a country, and IP is the best signal for one. A device's language says what somebody reads, not where they are, and plenty of phones in India are set to English (United States). So this is not an argument for a weaker guess — it is the observation that we are already holding the answer.

Every TMDB call goes through /api/tmdb, a Vercel Function on the platform this app already deploys to. Vercel attaches the requester's geolocation to every request that function receives:

api/tmdb.js — the function that already runs on every call
export default async function handler(request, response) {
  const country = request.headers['x-vercel-ip-country'];   // "IN"
  …
ipapi.co — todayx-vercel-ip-country — proposed
How it decidesThe visitor's IPThe visitor's IP. Identical technique.
Extra network requestOne, on every first loadNone. It rides a call the app already makes.
Who sees the IPA third partyOur own function, on our own deployment.
Blocked by a tracker blockerFrequentlyNo — it is a header on a first-party request
Can it hang or failYes, and it is awaited before the fallback runsNo. It is already in the response path.
Anything to discloseArguably yes, and there is nothingNo — nothing leaves the product
So this is not a trade-off. It is a strictly better version of the same feature. Same accuracy, one request fewer, one company fewer, and nothing to disclose. Checked against Vercel's own request-header reference: x-vercel-ip-country is a two-character ISO 3166-1 code sent to every Vercel Function, alongside x-vercel-ip-timezone.
Two cases the header does not cover, and both already have an answer. In local development there is no Vercel edge, so the header is absent — fall back to the device's time zone (Asia/Kolkata → IN), which is local, free, and a far better locator than language. And a VPN still lies, exactly as it does today — which is why the correction below matters whichever signal is used.

Guess quietly, ask at the point of use

A guess is fine when it is cheap and correctable. A guess is not fine when it silently decides what a person is told they can watch.

MomentBehaviour
First loadThe country arrives with the first TMDB call, from our own function. Nothing is asked, and nothing leaves the product.
First time a region-dependent thing is drawnWhere-to-watch on a title, or the find module's provider facet. The block appears with the country named and a one-tap Not in India? beside it. This is the find document's FB8, and this is where it lives.
Ever afterSettings. The current behaviour, minus the hint's claim about connections.
L5 — region is one value, held by the shell. useRegion is a hook with its own useState, and three screens call it, so three screens each hold their own copy synchronised by nothing but localStorage at mount. Change it in Settings and Discover keeps showing the old country until it happens to remount. One provider at the top, one value, every reader updated the moment it changes.

The theme is missing its default

The implementation is careful: dark is the product's default, nothing is stamped unless you choose, the OS preference decides when nothing is stamped, and restoreTheme() runs before first paint so a stored choice never flashes the other theme. All correct.

But the control offers two options and the system has three states. Dark, light — and follow the OS, which is what everybody gets until the first tap and nobody can get back to afterwards. On a phone that switches at sunset this is the difference between an app that behaves like the others and one that does not. Three segments: System · Light · Dark, with System as the default selection, and choosing it removes the stamp rather than writing a third value.
07

Saying what happened

The toast component is good — named rather than generic, six seconds, the timer restarting when the message changes so a run of quick actions never flashes the last one. Its own comment states the rule: “Breaking Bad · S2 E4 watched” is worth reading; “Saved” is not.

Two modules render it. Five screens can perform the action it describes.

Where you tap +What you are toldCan you undo it there?
LibraryA named toast with an undoYes
Title pageA named toast with an undoYes
DiscoverThe tile's badge changes. Nothing else.No
Search · BrowseThe tile's badge changes. Nothing else.No
Profile shelvesThe tile's badge changes. Nothing else.No
L6 — the toast belongs to the shell, and there is exactly one. Any screen can raise one; the shell decides where it sits and how long it lives. It already knows where: ui.css positions it above the tab bar and clears the safe area. Two modules owning one piece of furniture is how the same action ends up meaning two things.
The badge is not the problem — it is better than a toast. A poster that changes state under your thumb is feedback in exactly the right place. What is missing is the undo: today a mis-tap on a poster can only be corrected by opening the title or going to the library, because Tile deliberately ignores a second tap on a saved tile. The rule the title module already uses — tapping a ticked episode un-ticks it, so the control is its own undo — should hold here too, and then the toast is a courtesy rather than the only escape.

10

Decisions

IdQuestionMy recommendation
S1Is dropping ipapi.co a decision, or just a fix? Where-to-watch needs a country, and the concern was that removing the IP lookup would weaken it.A fix, and it makes the feature better rather than worse. The country still comes from the visitor's IP — it just comes from x-vercel-ip-country on our own function instead of from a third party, so it costs no request, cannot be blocked, cannot hang, and there is nothing to disclose. The only way this is a trade-off is if the app leaves Vercel, and then the fallback chain (time zone, then language) is the same one already in the file.
S2No mobile header — agreed? The alternative is a sticky 56px bar on every phone screen.No header. It would cost 9% of a 600px screen permanently to hold two controls people touch once. The You tab carries the account, Settings carries the theme, and .head-actions goes — once the You tab offers Sign in as well as Create an account.
S3How much scroll restoration? Position alone is cheap; position plus the pages a grid had loaded is not.Position everywhere, loaded pages on the two grids that page — search results and the library. Everywhere else a restored position on a re-fetched list is close enough, and pretending otherwise costs a cache nobody asked for.
S4Does the error reference go anywhere? A code on screen with nothing behind it is theatre.Show it anyway, log it locally. There is no error service and adding one is a real decision with a real bill. A short id the person can quote in a message is worth the four lines even with nothing behind it — and when a service does arrive, the id is already the thing it keys on.
And one retraction, which belongs here rather than buried in §02. The find document tells the build to delete .head-actions. That instruction is wrong as written — below 1120 those three controls are the only sign-in and the only theme control in the product. It is replaced by S2: delete them after the You tab offers both account actions, and not before.

Projector · shell · the frame

Every finding here was read out of the code on 18 September 2026 and checked in a running build at 390, 768 and 1280 — the missing boundary, the empty Suspense fallback, the chrome that stops at 1120, the third-party IP call, the three copies of the region, the theme with no way back to the system, and the toast that two modules own. Eight of the twenty states already ship and are named as such, because a document that only lists faults is not an audit, it is a complaint.
